# ABSTRACT

As higher education continues to expand in China, public safety management in densely populated campus areas has drawn growing attention. During emergencies such as fires or earthquakes, the ability to rapidly assess crowd conditions at each exit and guide orderly evacuation is critical for ensuring the safety of students and staff. Traditional video surveillance systems only provide passive recording without real-time analysis or active guidance capabilities. Infrared-sensor-based people counting solutions are limited by narrow coverage and an inability to distinguish movement directions, making them inadequate for intelligent monitoring in multi-exit campus scenarios.

To address these challenges, this thesis presents an intelligent campus crowd monitoring and early-warning system that integrates AI-based visual detection with physical sensor perception. The system adopts a dual-input complementary architecture: the remote AI channel captures video via an ESP32-CAM module and transmits it through MQTT to a PC running YOLOv8n deep learning inference (using COCO pre-trained weights) for accurate person detection and counting; the local emergency channel, built on an STM32F103 microcontroller with a flame sensor, performs fire detection independently without network dependency. These two channels coordinate bidirectionally through the MQTT message queue, combining the precision of intelligent analysis with the reliability of physical sensing.

Based on the three-channel parallel detection architecture, this thesis proposes a relative saturation-based four-strategy guidance algorithm. Based on the real-time congestion level and fire status of each exit channel, the algorithm adaptively selects among four response modes: "all clear" (no intervention needed), "guided" (recommend multiple exits ranked by saturation), "degraded" (advise phased passage when all safe exits are near capacity), and "emergency" (immediate evacuation when all active channels are on fire). A symmetric debouncing mechanism eliminates alarm oscillations near threshold boundaries. The coordinated decision results are sent via MQTT to each STM32 control node, driving LED indicators, buzzers, and SG90 servo-controlled gates for physical guidance, while DingTalk robot notifications deliver differentiated alarm messages, forming a complete closed loop of "detection → decision → execution → notification."

Test results demonstrate that the system operates stably in a three-channel environment, with all seven guidance algorithm test scenarios passing successfully. Key functions including alarm debouncing, device binding, and notification rate limiting all meet design expectations. This system provides a low-cost, scalable, and software-hardware integrated solution for campus emergency evacuation management.

**Key words**: crowd monitoring; YOLOv8; MQTT; emergency evacuation guidance; STM32
